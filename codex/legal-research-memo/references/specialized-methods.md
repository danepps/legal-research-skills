# Specialized Legal Research Methods

Use only the module relevant to the assignment. These rules supplement, rather than replace, the source and citation protocol.

## Statutory, regulatory, and administrative law

- Start with the text and version in force on the legally relevant date. Record enactment, amendment, effective, repeal, sunset, and transition dates separately.
- Map definitions, cross-references, exceptions, savings clauses, delegations, enforcement provisions, and related provisions in the same scheme before isolating a phrase.
- Trace the authority chain: constitution or organic statute, enacted statute, regulation, adjudication, guidance, manual, enforcement document, and informal statement. Label each source’s legal force.
- Check official notes, codification status, uncodified provisions, session laws, regulatory history, corrections, stays, and pending or final effective dates where relevant.
- Treat legislative and regulatory history as jurisdiction- and doctrine-dependent evidence. Identify speaker, stage, purpose, representativeness, and the interpretive use for which it is offered.
- For agency action, identify the action type, procedural vehicle, standard and scope of review, deference or interpretive framework current at the cutoff, exhaustion/finality/ripeness issues, and remedy.
- Distinguish announced policy from enforceable law and a proposal from a final rule. Verify currentness from an authoritative source.

## Comparative, foreign, and international law

- Define the forum, governing legal system, relevant hierarchy, and whether the question concerns domestic law, foreign law, treaty obligations, customary international law, or comparative persuasion.
- Build a jurisdiction-specific authority map rather than importing U.S. court hierarchy or terminology. Identify constitution, code or statute, regulation, controlling decisions, official commentary, and accepted secondary authorities for that system.
- Record the authoritative language, translation source, date/version, and whether the translation is official. Preserve the original-language term when translation could change the legal concept.
- Distinguish formal text, authoritative interpretation, institutional practice, and enforcement reality. Compare like with like across jurisdictions.
- Explain selection criteria for jurisdictions and functional equivalence of the rules compared. Do not generalize from a convenience sample.
- For treaties, check signature, ratification/accession, entry into force, reservations/declarations, amendments, relevant implementing law, authentic texts, and body-specific interpretive materials.
- Disclose language, database, access, and local-counsel limits. Use qualified review for conclusions that depend on unfamiliar foreign-law sources.

## Empirical and quantitative legal research

- State the legal and empirical questions separately. Define population, unit of analysis, sampling frame, period, jurisdiction, variables, outcome, denominator, missing data, and dataset provenance.
- Distinguish descriptive, associational, causal, predictive, and normative claims. Do not use a design to support a stronger type of claim than it can identify.
- Inspect coding rules, inter-coder reliability when relevant, measurement validity, comparison group, selection effects, uncertainty intervals, robustness checks, and revisions.
- Verify statistics against the original dataset, codebook, methods appendix, or official table when available. Recompute simple rates or comparisons when feasible and preserve the calculation.
- Treat significance, effect size, practical importance, external validity, and legal relevance as separate questions.
- Search for replications, corrections, retractions, alternative specifications, and incompatible definitions. Explain how doctrinal selection or publication practices may bias the observed sample.

## Record- and fact-intensive litigation research

- Separate record facts, allegations, findings, judicial notice, concessions, expert opinions, and lawyer argument. Cite the precise record source and docket location.
- Build an element-to-fact matrix: legal element or issue, supporting fact, contrary fact, evidentiary status, source, and missing proof.
- Track procedural posture and the corresponding treatment of facts, burdens, presumptions, and standards of review.
- Preserve chronology, provenance, and conflicts among declarations, exhibits, testimony, and orders. Do not resolve a factual dispute merely because one source is more detailed.
- Identify the facts that are outcome-determinative under each plausible legal rule and the next discovery or record source most likely to change the analysis.

## Method audit

Before delivery, confirm that the chosen module is named in the scope, its authority hierarchy or evidence design was followed, and any missing language, version, data, record, or implementation evidence is disclosed.
